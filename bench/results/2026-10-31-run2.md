# Chat benchmark: run 2, 2026-10-31

Campaign `benchmark` (DM) on the deployed app. I put the 23 questions in `bench/questions.md` to the app's assistant with the `ask_assistant` MCP tool, each in a fresh conversation (conversations 26–48), in order, one at a time. Every answer reported the model `claude-opus-5-5`. I graded per `bench/README.md` as strictly as the baseline (`2026-10-31-baseline.md`) was graded: one false or unsupported side claim makes an answer *wrong*, even when every expected fact is there. I checked doubtful claims against the sources with `search_reference`, `read_reference` and `stat_block`. For E1–E3, I re-ran each answer's ```encounter spec through the `encounter` tool and graded the card's stats as part of the answer.

What changed in the app since the baseline: the system prompt is cached across conversations; Markdown footnotes are inlined where they're cited; the assistant is told to state only what it read and to label its own suggestions; it records only what the user says happened; and a new `encounter` tool puts a fight together, with the card rendered from an ```encounter block.

## 5. Observations and next changes

**What moved.** The four targeted failures and the uninvited write are all fixed. Each fix is traceable to one change:
- **Footnote inlining** fixed N1, and gave F11 and E1 the right book.
- **"State only what you read"** fixed F7 and F11. Answers now say "(location not in my lookups)", "The book gives no check for the search", and "I couldn't rate the difficulty", where before they guessed.
- **"Record only what the user says happened"** fixed E2.

F8's fix came from better lookups, not better search: after the paraphrase search missed, the assistant tried `lookup("Bag of Devouring")`. Answers are shorter: mean output is down 19%, and down 43% on the encounter questions.

**What's left.** The two new wrong answers are the same kind of error the baseline had: a side claim, now an *overstatement* (F3's "only after a book") or an *unlabelled inference* (E1's "revivify is how"). They come from text the model paraphrased or reasoned from, not from text it invented. The prompt change caught fabricated locations and titles, but not "because", "only" or "how" statements. The other risk is E2's silent level-1 default, which made a confident difficulty claim from no data.

**Where the money goes now.** Cache reads are now the largest part of the cost (43%). Every API call re-reads the ~23k prefix plus the conversation so far, so the number of calls drives the cost. A 2-call answer costs about $0.025–0.047; a 5–6-call answer costs $0.08–0.14. Output is 26%, cache writes 31%, mostly per-answer lookup results and F1's cold start.

**Next changes most likely to help** (most impact first):
1. **Don't default the party level in `encounter`.** When the campaign has no PCs and no level is given, return `party: null` and no difficulty (as it already does with no `party_size`), or require `party_level` whenever `party_size` is passed. Tell the assistant to ask for the level instead of rating against an assumed one. This removes E2's misleading "well past Deadly". It's a one-line fix with no accuracy risk.
2. **Extend the grounding instruction to inferences and qualifiers.** For example: "an explanation of *why* or *how* something happens, or a word like *only*/*always*/*never*, needs the same support as a fact; if you're inferring, say so." This targets both remaining wrong answers. Optionally add a pre-send self-check: "list the claims not in your lookups; label or drop them."
3. **Cut API rounds by parallelising lookups.** Cost now scales with calls, not prefix writes. Encourage issuing independent lookups in one turn: search + read, or `encounter` + `stat_block` together. Or have `search_reference` return the top hit's full text, so search + read becomes one call. Going from about 3.7 to 2.5 calls per answer would cut about another 25–30%.
4. **Delete the baseline's leftover E2 journal entry (id 1) and the ended chat fact (50676) before the next run,** or rebuild the campaign. F5 and E2 both reported them as campaign state, which makes runs not quite comparable. The baseline's corpus defects remain too: the SRD Imp entry with the ice devil's actions, and the mis-nested SRD headings.
5. **Keep the paraphrase-search gap in view.** F8 passed this time because the assistant tried a `lookup` by item name, not because search improved: "actually disguised appears to be fake mimic" still found nothing useful. Tagging items as *disguised/cursed/trap* at digest time, or hybrid search, would make that question robust instead of lucky.

Rooms in the Chateau are randomized. **Each time a creature opens a room door from a hallway, roll 1d12.** The result is the room number (shown after each room title in House Layout) that's behind that door. *(Chateau Rousseau, Ch. 3: House Mechanics)*

### How it works
- **The party starts** in the Children's Room (**R1**) in the attic. They can walk freely between rooms and hallways. The only place they can't reach this way is Lynistad's room (**R★**).
- **A door stays linked to the rolled room until it closes.** After that:
  - If someone **in the hallway** opens it again, **roll again**.
  - If someone **in the room** opens it again, it leads back to **its normal hallway**.
- **Doors close softly within a minute** unless a creature or object holds them open. This makes it easy for the party to get split up.
- **Several doors opened at once:** to keep tracking manageable, any doors opened after the first lead to rooms **on the same floor as the first door**. This lasts until every door is closed.

- **Ghosts** ignore the randomization. They pass through doors and walls.
- **Mariner's Bow:** a creature attuned to it and looking through its scope opens a door to its **correct room**.

- **Lynistad's room (R★)** can never come up on the roll. Its walls and door are impenetrable, even to ghosts, and its door is magically warded.

### Hard limits
Doors, walls, ceilings and floors can't be destroyed or passed through by magic. Windows that face outside can't be opened or broken.
