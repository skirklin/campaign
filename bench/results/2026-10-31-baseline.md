## 4. Observations

**Failure patterns.** Every expected fact that was asked for was found except one (F8's Bag of Devouring). The errors are side claims: a floor, a section name, a book title. Each one is something the model added that the passage it read didn't say. The answers are long and pad themselves with extra context ("Also in the kitchen…", "Running notes", "the rest of his stats"). That padding is where all three false claims sit. Lookups were adequate in 22 of 23 answers. The two retrieval misses are (a) a paraphrase query against lexical search (F8) and (b) a footnote whose definition lives in a distant chunk (N1).

**Where the tokens and dollars go.** The usage numbers fit a price of about $0.40/M cache read, $5/M cache write and $20/M output; input is negligible.
- **Cache writes are 72% of all cost** ($2.99 of $4.18). Every fresh conversation writes at least **22,913 tokens** of prefix (S5, a single stat-block lookup, cost $0.129, of which about $0.115 was the write). Across 23 answers that fixed prefix is about 527k of the 598k cache-write tokens. Nothing in the prefix is reused between conversations, even ones started a few seconds apart, so the static part (system prompt plus tool definitions) seems to sit after, or mixed with, per-conversation content such as the campaign state.
- **Cache reads are 14%** ($0.59). Each extra API call re-reads the whole 22–30k context. That's cheap per call but scales with the number of rounds: E1 used 6 API calls and 127k cache-read tokens.
- **Output is 14%** ($0.60). The whole-encounter answers average 3k output tokens (E1 3.8k), mostly reprinted stat blocks.
- **Lookups themselves are small.** Each answer's write growth beyond the 22.9k floor is only about 1–7.6k tokens. Reading a passage, which by default returns the chunk plus one neighbour, adds roughly 1–1.5k tokens. A search adds about 0.3–0.8k, a stat block about 0.5–1.5k, and the outline about 1k. The biggest additions were F8 (+7.6k: outline, 3 searches, 2 reads), F1 (+7.5k: 4 reads) and E1 (+6.2k: 11 lookups). No single lookup is a token hog; the per-conversation prefix is.

**Changes most likely to help** (most impact first):
1. **Make the static prompt prefix cacheable across conversations.** Put the system prompt and tool definitions first, with a cache breakpoint, and move per-conversation or per-campaign state after it. Turning a 22.9k write ($0.115) into a read ($0.009) would cut the mean cost from about $0.18 to about $0.08 per answer (roughly −55%) with no change in accuracy.
2. **Resolve footnotes and citations when the source is digested.** Inline `[^⚙️]` → "(Source: *Eberron: Rising from the Last War*)" in each chunk that uses it, so the citation is in the passage the model reads. That fixes N1 and prevents the same guess for every other cross-book creature (warlock of the Archfey, the 🏺/👹 sources).
3. **Tell the assistant to state only what it read, and to keep extras short.** One instruction such as "don't add locations, section names or book titles you didn't read in a lookup; mark anything else as your own suggestion" targets all three wrong answers. Trimming unrequested sections ("Also in the kitchen", ring powers on a freezer question) would also cut output tokens.
4. **Add synonym or semantic matching to `search_reference`, or tag items when digesting.** F8's miss is a paraphrase ("not what it seems" against "appears to be … actually"). A hybrid or embedding search, or digest tags such as *disguised*, *cursed* and *trap* on items, would let one query find the Bag of Devouring, the rings and the candy bowl together.
5. **Don't write to the campaign from a question.** E2 logged a journal entry and a fact from what was a hypothetical prep question. Require an explicit "record this" before writing, at least in the Ask tab. Separately, the corpus has defects that `build_corpus.py` should fix: the SRD Imp entry contains the ice devil's actions, and SRD headings are mis-nested (every monster sits under *Modifying Creatures › A Legendary Creature's Lair*).

### F4. How does moving between rooms work in the Chateau?

Grade: **correct** · conversation 6 · 4 API calls · in 10 / cache read 68,172 / cache write 25,056 / out 1,019 · $0.1730 · 14.2 s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Each door is a random portal. The halls and stairs are fixed, but whenever someone opens a room door from a hallway, the room behind it is chosen at random. *(Chateau Rousseau, Ch. 3: House Mechanics. The source has no page numbers.)*

### The core rule
- **Opening a room door from a hallway: roll 1d12.** The result is the room number printed after each room title in House Layout (R1–R12), and that room is what's behind the door.
- **The link lasts until the door closes.** Then:
  - **Reopened from the hallway:** roll again for a new room.
  - **Reopened from inside the room:** it leads back to that room's own hallway.
- **Doors close softly by themselves within 1 minute** unless a creature or object holds them open.
- **Moving between hallways and stairs is free.** Only room doors are randomised.

### Things to watch
- **The party can get split up.** If the door shuts behind some of them, the ones still in the hall will roll a new room when they open it again. The book warns about this directly.
- **More than one door open at once:** after the first door, any other door opened leads to a room on the **same floor as the first door**. This lasts until every door is closed. It's there to make tracking easier for you.
- **Kitchen (R9) and Dining Room:** the only pair of rooms joined directly. The east door between them **doesn't trigger randomisation**.

### Ways around it
- **Ghosts** (Anni, Neta, Ulvaeus) ignore the system. They phase through walls.
- **Mariner's Bow:** a creature **attuned** to it and looking through its scope opens doors to the **correct** room. Ulvaeus in the Game Room gives it to anyone who beats him at billiards or in combat.
- **Lynistad's Room (R★)** never comes up on the d12. It can't be reached by randomisation at all, and its walls and door stop everything, ghosts included. Neta sells information about its secret entrance.

### What doesn't work
- Doors, walls, floors and ceilings **can't be destroyed or passed through magically**.
- Windows facing outside **can't be opened or broken**. Through them the party sees the house drifting in a green and purple mist.

### F6. What happens if a player character dies in the Chateau?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A PC who dies in the Chateau **comes straight back as a spirit**. They aren't out of the adventure. The module lists this as a *suggested* house rule (Chateau Rousseau, Chapter 2: Suggested House Rules), so you can choose whether to use it.

### When they return as a spirit
- They **get back all spell slots**, and their other features recover as if they'd taken a **Long Rest**.
- Their **Hit Point maximum is halved**.
- Their **creature type becomes Undead**. This matters for things like *turn undead* and radiant damage, and for anything that targets or protects against undead.
- **Goren has trouble seeing spirits.** When he tries to find or attack a spirit, the spirit counts as being in a **Heavily Obscured** area.

### If the spirit drops to 0 HP
The character is **destroyed for good**. Only divine intervention, *Wish* or something similarly powerful can bring them back.

### At the end
If the party **escapes the Chateau or defeats Goren**, every spirit that wasn't destroyed **comes back to life** and leaves with the others.

The book gives this rule because the house has no safe places and Goren is actively hunting the party. It pairs it with more generous starting gear, which is also suggested:
- **500 gp + 1d10 × 25 gp**
- **one Common and one Uncommon magic item** per PC
- attuning to an item takes **1 minute** instead of a Short Rest
